Literature search guide · Electronic Engineering

PICO, SPIDER and PCC

How to turn a vague research proposal on SDR-based FM commercial monitoring in Colombia into concepts you can search. The three frameworks differ in the type of question you ask and the type of studies you are looking for.
01 · The frameworks

Three frameworks, three kinds of question

Quantitative · performance
PICO
For questions of effect or performance: does technique X detect, measure or improve Y? In engineering, it fits system tests, benchmarks and controlled comparisons.
P
Population
What system, signal or dataset do you test on?
I
Intervention
Which method or technology is applied?
C
Comparison
Compared with what? (Can be omitted.)
O
Outcome
Which metric do you measure: accuracy, recall, latency?
Verb cue: “Effect of …”, “Performance of …”
Qualitative · mixed methods
SPIDER
For questions about how people work with, perceive or experience something. PICO fits these studies poorly: they have no “intervention” or “comparison”.
S
Sample
Who takes part?
PI
Phenomenon of Interest
The experience or phenomenon.
D
Design
Type of study: interviews, case study, focus groups.
E
Evaluation
What is evaluated: perceptions, trust, barriers.
R
Research type
Qualitative or mixed methods.
Verb cue: “Experiences of …”
Scoping reviews
PCC
The most flexible framework. Use it when the topic is broad or poorly defined and you want to map what has been studied, not answer a closed question.
P
Population
Who or what?
C
Concept
The core concept or technology.
C
Context
Where, or in what setting: country, sector, regulatory system.
Verb cue: “What is known about …”
02 · Worked example

One vague idea, three different questions

“I want to use software-defined radio to monitor commercials on FM stations and check compliance in Colombia.”
PICO
Does SDR-based audio fingerprinting detect FM ad spots more accurately than manual listening logs?
P · FM broadcasts (88–108 MHz) from Bogotá stations
I · RTL-SDR receiver + audio fingerprinting
C · manual listening logs
O · precision, recall, missed spots
SPIDER
How do station managers and regulatory staff experience automated ad monitoring as compliance evidence?
S · station managers, ANE / MinTIC inspectors
PI · automated monitoring as compliance evidence
D · interviews, focus groups
E · perceptions, trust, barriers
R · qualitative
PCC
What is known about SDR-based broadcast monitoring for regulatory compliance in Colombia and Latin America?
P · FM broadcasters and spectrum regulators
C · SDR-based ad and broadcast monitoring
C · Colombia, Latin America
03 · Practical steps

Six steps for a vague proposal

1
Choose the framework by the verb of your question
“Performance of” → PICO. “Experiences of” → SPIDER. “What is known about” → PCC.
2
Fill in only what you already know
With a vague proposal, having 2 of 4 elements is normal. Use the gaps to decide what you still need to specify, such as the receiver hardware or the metric.
3
List synonyms for each element
For Concept: SDR, software-defined radio, RTL-SDR, USRP, plus Spanish equivalents (radio definida por software, monitoreo de radiodifusión, cuñas radiales).
4
Join them with Boolean operators
Use OR between synonyms within an element, and AND between the elements.
5
Start with few elements
In vague searches, combine only 2 or 3 concepts (SDR + FM, or FM + Colombia) instead of requiring every element at once.
6
Use the results to refine your thesis
The articles show what is saturated (signal processing), what is missing (Colombian compliance cases), and which context is understudied. Your final question usually appears there.
Example search string
(SDR OR "software-defined radio" OR RTL-SDR OR USRP)
AND ("FM broadcast*" OR "radio broadcast*")
AND (advertis* OR commercial* OR "audio fingerprint*")
AND (Colombia OR "Latin America" OR compliance OR regulat*)
OR inside an element widens the search. AND between elements narrows it.
